## Question one: is there a store-specific pickup price?

Quoted verbatim, mailbox addresses removed:

> For question one: no, we do not currently expose a separate store-specific
> pickup price for Walmart.
>
> The Walmart scraper returns the price available on the Walmart product page
> for the provided store/ZIP context. In practice, this is the online price
> shown by Walmart, even when the store ID and ZIP are applied and pickup
> availability is resolved.
>
> The `store_id` and `zip_code` affect the store context, assortment, pickup
> availability, stock, and related pickup fields, but walmart does not have a
> separate field that returns a distinct in-store or pickup-specific store
> price.
>
> So if your requirement is specifically "the price a shopper would pay in one
> physical store for pickup", and that differs from the online page price, we
> do not currently provide that as a separate output field in this dataset
> because this is not presented by walmart.

## Question two: is the unit price displayed or calculated?

> For question two: yes, the `unit_price` / `unit` value is taken from the
> Walmart page data shown by Walmart, not calculated by us from `final_price`
> and size.
>
> The scraper extracts the unit price information from the text on the page,
> text is generated by site's script and our scraper collects this text and
> put it in the respective field.

## What this does and does not establish

**Does.** The provider states that the per-unit rate originates in text Walmart
renders on the page, not in arithmetic the provider performs. That answers the
objection, raised in our own enquiry, that `20.84 / 132 fl oz = 0.1579` against
a returned `0.158` looked like a calculation. A retailer-computed display value
matches its own arithmetic; that match is not evidence of who computed it.

**Does not.** This is the provider's account of their own pipeline, not an
independent observation. It does not establish the displayed *format*. The
rows hold `unit_price` as a bare number and `unit` as a separate string, for
example `0.057` and `fl oz`. No captured field holds the string a page
renders.

Two page observations exist, and both are at the wrong store:

| Product | Page shows | Provider row | Store on the page |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ol' Roy dog food | `86.8 ¢/lb` at $3.47 | `0.868` / `lb` | Sacramento, not 100 |
| Xtra Tropical Passion 100.8 fl oz | `5.7 ¢/fl oz` at $5.78 | `0.057` / `fl oz` | Sacramento, not 100 |

The first is recorded in this repository. The second was observed on
1 November 2026 as a browser screenshot held outside the repository; it is
cited here as an observation, not as a captured artefact, and an attempt to
repeat it with store 100 selected failed because Walmart's store picker
returned a technical error.

So the rendered notation is cents with the ¢ sign, on both observations, and
neither is at store 100. Composing a string from `unit_price` and `unit`
therefore means choosing a notation the capture does not hold, and the units
present include `count`, `ea` and `yd`, whose displayed forms have not been
observed at all.

It also does not establish anything about store 100 specifically. The live page
observations available so far resolved to a Sacramento store, and
`STORE_CONTEXT_FINDING.md` records that those figures must not be presented as
store 100's.

**Therefore `ppm` stays blank** until either the provider supplies the rendered
string, or a page observation at store 100 establishes the displayed form, or
the client accepts a value explicitly labelled as composed from two captured
numeric fields rather than captured as displayed.
